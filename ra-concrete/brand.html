Brand guidelines · v1.0

Two slabs. One joint.

The R&A Concrete identity is built on the geometry of the work itself: poured panels, saw-cut control joints and the chamfered edge left by a good set of forms. It is meant to read cleanly on a phone screen, a truck door, a shirt and a yard sign.

01 — Direction

Architectural concrete.

Precision, permanence and craft. Instead of the usual contractor cues (trucks, trowels, hard hats), the identity borrows from finished architectural concrete: calm gray and ivory surfaces, sharp edges and deliberate joint lines.

Warm, not cold. Warm ivory replaces stark white, and a single iron-oxide accent ties to rebar, pigmented concrete and Georgia red clay. The result feels approachable for a homeowner and serious for a general contractor.

Specification language. Small monospace labels act like drawing annotations. They set up expectations the way a set of plans does, and they let the site mark provisional content honestly without looking unfinished.

02 — Logo exploration

Three concepts, one direction.

Selected

A · Split slab

Two poured panels divided by a saw-cut joint, with R and A cut straight through. The partnership in the name becomes the structure of the mark. It’s solid, reads at 16 px and cuts cleanly in one color of vinyl or thread.

Explored

B · Board-form

Letters set in horizontal striations like board-formed concrete. It has plenty of texture, but the gaps fill in when small and embroider poorly. Set aside as a pattern idea, not a logo.

Explored

C · Chamfered frame

An oversized ampersand in a form-edge frame. It’s elegant at large sizes, but the initials become secondary and the thin frame disappears on a favicon or a shirt.

03 — Logo system

Lockups and color versions.

Clear space

Keep space equal to one quarter of the mark’s height clear on every side (the distance from the top of the slab to the top of the letters).

Minimum size

  • Horizontal: 40 px tall on screen, 0.5 in in print
  • Stacked: 72 px tall on screen, 1 in in print
  • Mark alone: 16 px / 0.25 in
  • Embroidery: mark at least 1 in wide; use the mark alone below 3 in

Don’t

  • Stretch, skew, rotate or outline the logo
  • Recolor the ampersand anything other than oxide or the logo’s own color
  • Add shadows, bevels, gradients or concrete textures to the logo
  • Place it on busy photos without a solid panel behind it
  • Rebuild the wordmark in another typeface
04 — Color

A restrained palette.

05 — Typography

One family, one utility face.
