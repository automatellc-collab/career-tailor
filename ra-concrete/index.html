04 — Why it matters

What you see is the last step. What’s under it matters more.

Good concrete comes down to preparation, timing and finishing. Five things decide whether a slab lasts.

Ask us how each one applies to your project: (470) 392-0670

  • The base

    Most concrete problems start below the surface, in soil, grade and drainage.

  • The joints

    Concrete moves as it cures. Control joints decide where that movement shows.

  • The finish

    Square forms, clean edges and an even finish from one end of the pour to the other.

  • The weather

    Rain, heat and cold all affect a pour, so timing matters as much as technique.

  • The cure

    New concrete keeps gaining strength for weeks. Early care protects the surface.

07 — FAQ

Straight answers before you ask for a number.

Something not covered here? Call, text or email and ask.

How much will my project cost?

It depends on the size of the area, site access, whether old concrete has to be removed, how much base preparation is needed, thickness and reinforcement, and the finish you choose. An accurate price depends on seeing the site, so start with a call or an estimate request.

Do you remove old concrete?

Many replacement jobs start with tear-out and haul-off. Mention existing concrete in your request so it’s included in the assessment.

Owner to confirm tear-out & disposal services
When can I walk or drive on new concrete?

It depends on the mix, the thickness, temperature and humidity. Concrete keeps gaining strength for weeks after it’s poured, so there’s no one-size-fits-all number. Ask what applies to your pour.

Will my concrete crack?

Honestly, concrete can crack. It shrinks as it cures and moves with temperature and the ground below. Good base preparation, correct joint spacing and proper curing go a long way toward controlling where cracking happens and keeping it minor.

What happens if it rains on pour day?

Weather has a real effect on concrete. Pour dates are planned around the forecast and can move if conditions would put the finish at risk.

Do I need a permit?

Permit requirements vary by city, county and project type. Driveway aprons and work near the street often have their own rules. Ask us what applies to your location.
